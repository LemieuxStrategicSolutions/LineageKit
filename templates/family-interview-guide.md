# Family Interview Guide

## Before the interview

- Ask permission to record.
- Identify photographs, certificates, letters, funeral cards, military papers, family Bibles, and labeled objects the person may have.
- Bring the names and dates already known, but do not lead the witness.

## Questions

1. What are your earliest memories of your parents and grandparents?
2. What names, nicknames, maiden names, or alternate spellings did relatives use?
3. Where did the family live, and why did they move?
4. What work did people do?
5. What churches, schools, clubs, unions, teams, or military units mattered?
6. What traditions, foods, sayings, holidays, or stories were passed down?
7. Who was close to whom? Who became estranged?
8. Were there adoptions, remarriages, children who died young, or relatives known by another surname?
9. Which family photographs can you identify?
10. Who else should be interviewed next?

## After the interview

- Save the recording and transcript.
- Separate first-hand memories from stories heard from someone else.
- Create research questions from claims that can be checked in records.
- Record the interview itself as a source.
