## Phase 1 — Prove the direct lines through the great-great-grandparents

Work from the present backward. The governing priority is the direct ancestral line: prove each parent, then that parent's parents, before expanding sideways. Siblings of direct ancestors are secondary unless they help break the direct-line problem.

**Foundational goal:** establish every direct ancestral line for {{ROOT_PEOPLE}} through all great-great-grandparents. Imported seed relationships are inventory only until independently supported.

### Name-variant and Americanization protocol

Every direct ancestor must be searched under original-language, church-Latin, phonetic, translated, and Americanized forms. A name variant is a search path, not proof of identity. Two records with different names may be joined only when at least one independent bridge also agrees, such as spouse, parents, child, exact birth date, address, occupation, parish, immigration contact, or a distinctive migration sequence.

## Phase 2 — Branch-by-branch expansion

Start with one surname-couple at a time. For each couple:

- prove both sets of parents
- identify every child, including those who died young
- follow siblings through marriage and death
- map residences and migration
- search collateral relatives who can break dead ends
- document name variants, dit names, anglicizations, and spelling shifts

Initial branch containers: {{LINE_SURNAMES}}. Their actual priority order will be set after the baseline audit.

## Phase 3 — Story enrichment

For proven people, research the life around the dates: occupation and workplace · military service · church and community life · property and neighborhood · education · migration and historical context · illnesses or causes of death only when appropriate and documented · newspaper mentions, photographs, correspondence, and family recollections.

## Phase 4 — DNA-assisted research

Use DNA only with explicit consent and a written question. Cluster matches by shared ancestors, distinguish maternal and paternal groups, and document the reasoning chain. DNA is supporting evidence, not a license to expose living relatives or unexpected parentage casually.

## Phase 5 — Publication and preservation

Create branch reports, person profiles, timelines, source packages, photo captions, and a private family narrative. Maintain exportable data so {{USER_NAME}} can later choose a website, book, archive, or selective merge back to {{TREE_HOST}}.

## Progress measures

Track quality and discovery separately: new people with documented relationships · existing people materially enriched · vital events supported by records · source images and complete citations saved · contradictions resolved · interviews completed · photographs identified · research questions answered · hypotheses disproved before they contaminated the tree.
