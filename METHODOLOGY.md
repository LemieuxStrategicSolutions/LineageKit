# METHODOLOGY — the research practice

This is the methodology the templates encode. BOOTSTRAP generates a private `RESEARCH-PLAN.md` from it; this copy is the reference edition.

## The phase model

**Phase 0 — Preserve and inventory.** Secure a read-only seed export from the existing tree host (if any), checksum it, and import it into staging with every claim marked `hypothesis`. Inventory names, date ranges, places, surnames, source coverage, missing vital facts, duplicates, and contradictions. Identify living people and restrict their sensitive information from day one. Produce a baseline quality report *before* adding new research — you cannot measure progress without it.

**Phase 1 — Prove the direct lines.** Work from the present backward. The governing priority is the direct ancestral line: prove each parent, then that parent's parents, before expanding sideways. The foundational milestone is every direct ancestor through the great-great-grandparents — up to sixteen positions per root person. Siblings of direct ancestors are recorded when they help prove identity, parentage, maiden name, locality, or migration; otherwise they wait. Imported seed relationships are inventory, not proof, until independently supported.

**Phase 2 — Branch-by-branch expansion.** One surname-couple at a time: prove both sets of parents, identify every child (including those who died young), follow siblings through marriage and death, map residences and migration, search collateral relatives who can break dead ends, and document name variants and spelling shifts.

**Phase 3 — Story enrichment.** For *proven* people only, research the life around the dates: occupation, military service, church and community life, property, education, migration context, newspaper mentions, photographs, correspondence, recollections. Causes of death only when appropriate and documented. The story is written after the facts are stable, never before.

**Phase 4 — DNA-assisted research (consent-gated).** DNA only with explicit consent and a written research question. Cluster matches by shared ancestors, distinguish maternal and paternal groups, document the reasoning chain. DNA is supporting evidence — never a license to expose living relatives or unexpected parentage casually.

**Phase 5 — Publication and preservation.** Branch reports, person profiles, timelines, source packages, photo captions, the public archive site. Maintain exportable data so the owner can later choose a website, book, archive, or a selective merge back to their tree host — a merge that happens only as a deliberate, human-approved package, never as sync.

## The evidence hierarchy

When sources compete, prefer, in order:

1. Civil birth, marriage, and death records
2. Church baptism, marriage, and burial records
3. Census and residence records
4. Obituaries, cemetery records, and probate
5. Immigration, naturalization, passenger, and border records
6. Military records
7. Land, directory, employment, newspaper, school, and organization records
8. Oral histories, photographs, letters, Bibles, and family papers

And within any record type, prefer: original record image created near the event → official certificate or archival copy → reliable derivative with a clear citation → index or transcription → obituary/newspaper/cemetery database/published genealogy → another person's online tree → unsourced family lore. Lower-ranked sources are valuable *leads*; they simply require independent confirmation.

## The name-variant protocol

Every ancestor is searched under original-language, church-Latin, phonetic, translated, and Americanized forms. Examples of the pattern (illustrative, not exhaustive):

- Jan / John / Joannes / Ivan — where the record jurisdiction's language supports it
- Władysław / Wladyslaw / Ladislaus / Walter — with lower-confidence substitutions (e.g. William) flagged as such
- Guillaume / William; Pierre / Peter; Marija / Maria / Mary / Marie
- Surnames cross-searched across phonetic and orthographic families, including feminine forms and diacritic-stripped spellings

**The rule that keeps this safe: a name variant is a search path, not proof of identity.** Two records with different names may be joined only when at least one independent bridge also agrees — spouse, parents, child, exact birth date, address, occupation, parish, immigration contact, or a distinctive migration sequence. Variants expand the candidate pool; documentary context decides whether the people are identical. Beware of empirical aliases (a person who consistently appears under a name that is *not* a linguistic equivalent) — record them as observed facts about that person, not as general translations.

## Search discipline

- Search by place, relatives, occupation, and date range as well as name.
- Work backward from the most recent available census and reconcile the *entire household*.
- Inspect the record image; never rely only on an index.
- **Record negative searches** in the research log with the same care as finds — a closed dead end is a result.
- Separate facts *stated by* the record from conclusions *inferred from* it.
- Check the existing research log and source register before searching — never repeat a logged search.

## The dead-end worksheet

When a line stalls, fill out a structured worksheet before more searching: the exact question · known facts with sources · current assumptions (each one challengeable) · name variants in play · associates and witnesses (the "FAN club": friends, associates, neighbors) · place and jurisdiction changes over time · searches already done including negatives · the single best next record set. Most "brick walls" are an unexamined assumption plus an unsearched record set.

## The expansion rule

Do not chase the largest possible tree. Depth of evidence on the direct lines beats breadth of unsourced names, every time. A 200-person tree where every claim is labeled and sourced outlives a 5,000-person name pile.

## Progress measures

Track quality and discovery separately: new people with documented relationships · existing people materially enriched · vital events newly supported by records · source images and complete citations saved · contradictions resolved · interviews completed · photographs identified · research questions answered · **hypotheses disproved before they contaminated the tree** (yes, that last one is a win).
